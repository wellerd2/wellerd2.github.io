Methods

The MODES Lab uses quantitative, epidemiologic, and translational methods to connect environmental and agri-food system processes to public health outcomes.

Data Science Frameworks for Food System Decision Support

Across all research areas, data science provides a unifying framework for integrating environmental, microbiological, behavioral, and epidemiological data. Our work develops and adapts analytical tools that reflect real-world complexity and uncertainty, enabling the translation of diverse data streams into actionable insights. These frameworks are designed to support decision-making by farmers, regulators, and public health practitioners across the food system.

Public Health and Epidemiologic Methods

We use epidemiologic and quantitative methods to understand how food and environmental hazards translate into human illness and to generate evidence that can guide prevention. Depending on the question, this includes regression modeling, counterfactual approaches, surveillance data analysis, source attribution, and hierarchical or spatial models.

Integrating Diverse Data Streams

Understanding food safety risks requires evidence that spans scales, from microbial processes in the environment to population-level health outcomes. Our research integrates field-based sampling, laboratory analyses, surveillance data, observational datasets, and epidemiologic information to better understand how hazards emerge, persist, and affect human health across agri-food systems.

Systems Modeling and Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

Food safety challenges often involve competing goals related to environmental protection, economic viability, and public health. We use systems modeling and decision-analytic approaches to quantify interactions, feedbacks, trade-offs, and synergies across agri-food systems.

Stakeholder-Centered and Translational Research

Our research is grounded in stakeholder-defined needs and emphasizes collaboration throughout the research process. By working closely with industry partners, government agencies, growers, and communities, we co-develop studies and tools that are scientifically robust and practically relevant.
