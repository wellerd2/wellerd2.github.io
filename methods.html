Methods

Flexible, Integrative Approaches

The MODES Lab uses a flexible, integrative approach to analyze food safety risks across complex systems. Rather than relying on a single method, our work combines epidemiologic, statistical, spatial, and computational approaches within a data science framework. We develop and adapt analytical tools that reflect real-world complexity and uncertainty, enabling the integration of diverse data streams into actionable insights for decision-making across the food system.

Leveraging Diverse Data Streams

Understanding food safety risks requires evidence that spans scales, from microbial processes in the environment to population-level health outcomes. Our research leverages field-based sampling, laboratory analyses, and observational datasets, including environmental monitoring and epidemiological information, to capture these connections. This integrative approach allows us to link conditions on the ground to public health outcomes and downstream impacts across the food system.

Systems Modeling and Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

Food safety challenges often involve competing goals related to environmental protection, economic viability, and public health. We use systems modeling approaches, including methods designed to quantify interactions, feedbacks, and trade-offs, to evaluate how interventions affect outcomes across agri-food systems. By explicitly linking ecological processes, management decisions, and human health risks, this work supports more balanced and transparent decision-making.

Stakeholder-Centered and Translational Research

Our research is grounded in stakeholder-defined needs and emphasizes collaboration throughout the research process. By working closely with industry partners, government agencies, and communities, we co-develop studies and tools that are scientifically robust and practically relevant. This translational approach ensures that insights generated through ecological, epidemiological, and data-driven research can inform real-world food safety and public health policy and practice.
