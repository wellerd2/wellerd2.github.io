How the lab works

Research Methods

Methods in the MODES Lab are chosen to support public health interpretation, not just technical sophistication. The goal is to integrate diverse evidence streams, make uncertainty visible, and produce analyses that are actually useful for action.

Public Health Data Science and Decision Support

Across research areas, the lab uses data science as a public health tool for integrating environmental, microbiological, behavioral, and epidemiologic data. Analytical workflows are designed to reflect real-world complexity and uncertainty while translating diverse evidence into actionable insights for prevention, surveillance, and decision-making.

Integrating Diverse Data Streams Across Public Health and Agri-Food Systems

Understanding food safety risk requires evidence that spans scales, from microbial processes in the environment to population-level health outcomes. The lab integrates field-based sampling, laboratory analyses, surveillance data, observational datasets, and epidemiologic information to better understand how hazards emerge, persist, and affect human health across agri-food systems.

Epidemiologic and Quantitative Methods for Risk Inference

Depending on the question, the lab uses regression modeling, hierarchical and spatial methods, machine learning, source attribution approaches, systems modeling, and decision-analytic frameworks to identify drivers of risk, evaluate interventions, and support inference across complex systems.

These methods are used not simply for prediction, but to support clearer public health interpretation and more transparent decision-making under uncertainty.

Systems Modeling and Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

Food safety challenges often involve competing goals related to environmental protection, economic viability, and public health. The lab uses systems modeling approaches to quantify interactions, feedbacks, and trade-offs so that interventions can be evaluated in relation to multiple outcomes at once.

Stakeholder-Centered and Translational Research

Research in the MODES Lab is grounded in stakeholder-defined needs and emphasizes collaboration throughout the research process. By working with industry partners, government agencies, growers, and communities, the lab aims to co-develop studies and tools that are both scientifically robust and practically relevant.
