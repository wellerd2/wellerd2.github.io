Methods

The MODES Lab uses quantitative and translational methods that connect environmental and agri-food system processes to public health outcomes.

Public Health Data Science and Decision Support

Across research areas, the lab uses data science as a public health tool for integrating environmental, microbiological, behavioral, and epidemiologic data. Our goal is not only prediction, but clearer interpretation and more useful decision support.

Integrating Diverse Data Streams Across Public Health and Agri-Food Systems

Understanding food safety risks requires evidence that spans scales, from field conditions and microbial processes to population-level health outcomes. We integrate field-based sampling, laboratory analyses, surveillance data, observational datasets, and epidemiologic information to better understand how hazards emerge, persist, and affect human health.

Epidemiologic and Quantitative Methods for Risk Inference

Depending on the question, this includes regression modeling, hierarchical and spatial approaches, machine learning, systems modeling, and decision-analytic frameworks. These methods are used to identify drivers of risk, evaluate interventions, and support inference across complex systems.

Systems Modeling and Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis

Many food safety challenges involve competing goals related to public health, environmental protection, feasibility, and economic viability. We use systems-based and decision-analytic approaches to quantify interactions, feedbacks, and trade-offs and to support more transparent decision-making under uncertainty.

Stakeholder-Centered and Translational Research

Our work is grounded in stakeholder-defined needs and emphasizes collaboration throughout the research process. By working closely with growers, industry partners, government agencies, and communities, we aim to produce work that is both rigorous and useful.
