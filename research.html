Research

Environmental Drivers of Foodborne Risk

We study how land use, water quality, weather, and farm and food practices affect pathogen dynamics and dispersal from farm-to-fork, and what this means in terms of human and animal health risks.

Epidemiology, Surveillance, and Attribution

We use surveillance data to understand patterns of illness, how they change over time, where infections come from, and how risk is distributed across populations, including work on attribution, disparities, and links to environmental and exposure pathways.

Modeling, Quantitative Approaches, and Risk Assessment

We use statistical, spatial, and machine learning approaches to analyze environmental and surveillance data. This includes identifying drivers of contamination, integrating data across systems, modeling pathways and interactions, supporting prediction and prioritization, and applying quantitative microbial risk assessment to link contamination and exposure to potential health impacts.

Decision Support, Co-Management, and Risk Integration

We study how microbial risks are managed in systems where stakeholders face competing priorities, including environmental goals, resource constraints, and multiple hazards. This includes how monitoring data are interpreted, how risk varies within systems, and how management strategies can be adapted to specific settings.

Interventions and Mitigation

We evaluate practical approaches to reducing contamination, including testing specific interventions, assessing practices under real-world conditions, and comparing strategies across environments and systems.
