Our Research

Research themes

The lab uses food safety as a lens to study how agri-food systems function, fail, and change.

Ecology & control of pathogenic and spoilage organisms

Pathogenic and spoilage organisms move through agri-food systems via interconnected ecological and epidemiological processes shaped by environmental conditions, agricultural practices, and human behavior. Our research examines how these organisms persist, spread, and interact with food production environments through processing and distribution, and to human and animal illness.

By linking microbial ecology with epidemiology and the development of novel control strategies, we aim to support effective, context-sensitive approaches to managing food safety risks across the food system.

Foodborne Disease Epidemiology

A central focus of the MODES Lab is understanding how foodborne hazards translate into human illness and how epidemiologic evidence can be used to guide prevention. Our work examines the distribution and determinants of foodborne disease across populations, production systems, and environments, with attention to how risk is shaped by ecological, behavioral, and structural factors.

By integrating epidemiologic methods with environmental, microbiological, and food systems data, we aim to generate evidence that supports more effective surveillance, source attribution, intervention design, and policy. This work reflects the lab’s broader commitment to linking upstream conditions in agri-food systems to downstream public health outcomes.

Building safe, circular food systems

Efforts to reduce food loss and waste and to promote circular resource use increasingly intersect with food safety goals. Interventions designed to improve safety or sustainability can create unintended ecological, economic, or health consequences when viewed in isolation.

We apply systems-based approaches to evaluate trade-offs and synergies among food safety, environmental sustainability, and circular food system objectives, with the goal of supporting strategies that protect public health while maintaining ecosystem services and long-term system viability.

Anticipating & managing climate change impacts on agri-food systems

Climate change is altering the environmental conditions that govern microbial behavior, exposure pathways, and human responses across agri-food systems. Shifts in temperature, precipitation, extreme weather, and infrastructure reliability can increase contamination risks, illness, recalls, and spoilage.

Our research focuses on understanding these climate-driven dynamics across the food system and on developing forecasting and early-warning tools that link environmental change to food safety and public health outcomes.

Advancing food system equity

Foodborne illness and food system disruptions disproportionately affect under-resourced and marginalized communities, reflecting inequities in infrastructure, exposure, and access to safe food. Our research integrates epidemiological data with environmental, food system, and social information to identify disparities in risk and outcomes across populations.

By examining how structural factors shape exposure and vulnerability, from production through consumption, we seek to support interventions and policies that improve food safety while advancing equity and resilience.
