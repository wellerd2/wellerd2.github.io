Our research

Research

The lab’s research agenda is organized around how hazards move through agri-food systems, how those risks become human illness, and how decisions can better account for public health, sustainability, and equity at the same time.

Ecology & Control of Pathogenic & Spoilage Organisms

Pathogenic and spoilage organisms move through agri-food systems via interconnected ecological and epidemiological processes shaped by environmental conditions, agricultural practices, and human behavior. The lab studies how these organisms persist, spread, and interact with production environments through processing, distribution, and ultimately human and animal illness.

By linking microbial ecology with epidemiology and the development of novel control strategies, this work aims to generate context-sensitive approaches to managing food safety risk across the food system.

Foodborne Disease Epidemiology

A central focus of the MODES Lab is understanding how foodborne hazards translate into human illness and how epidemiologic evidence can guide prevention. The lab examines the distribution and determinants of foodborne disease across populations, geographies, and production systems, with attention to how risk is shaped by ecological, behavioral, and structural factors.

By integrating epidemiologic methods with environmental, microbiological, and food systems data, this work supports surveillance, source attribution, intervention design, and policy. It also anchors the lab’s broader commitment to linking upstream conditions in agri-food systems to downstream public health outcomes.

Building Safe, Circular Food Systems

Efforts to reduce food loss and waste and promote circular resource use increasingly intersect with food safety goals. Yet interventions designed to improve safety or sustainability can create unintended ecological, economic, or health consequences when viewed in isolation.

The lab applies systems-based approaches to evaluate trade-offs and synergies among food safety, environmental sustainability, and circular food system objectives, with the aim of identifying strategies that protect public health while maintaining ecosystem services and long-term system viability.

Anticipating & Managing Climate Change Impacts on Agri-Food Systems

Climate change is altering the environmental conditions that govern microbial behavior, exposure pathways, and human responses across agri-food systems. Shifts in temperature, precipitation, extreme weather, and infrastructure reliability can increase contamination risks, illness, recalls, and spoilage.

This research focuses on understanding those climate-driven dynamics and on developing forecasting and early-warning approaches that link environmental change to food safety and public health outcomes.

Advancing Food System Equity

Foodborne illness and food system disruptions disproportionately affect under-resourced and marginalized communities, reflecting inequities in infrastructure, exposure, and access to safe food. The lab integrates epidemiologic data with environmental, food system, and social information to identify disparities in risk and outcomes across populations.

By examining how structural factors shape exposure and vulnerability from production through consumption, this work seeks to support interventions and policies that improve food safety while advancing equity and resilience.
