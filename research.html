Research

Ecology & Control of Pathogenic & Spoilage Organisms

Pathogenic and spoilage organisms move through agri-food systems via interconnected ecological and epidemiological processes shaped by environmental conditions, agricultural practices, and human behavior. Our research examines how these organisms persist, spread, and interact with food production environments through processing and distribution, and to human and animal illness.

Foodborne Disease Epidemiology

A central focus of the MODES Lab is understanding how foodborne hazards translate into human illness and how epidemiologic evidence can be used to guide prevention. We examine the distribution and determinants of foodborne disease across populations, production systems, and environments, with attention to how risk is shaped by ecological, behavioral, and structural factors.

Building Safe, Circular Food Systems

Efforts to reduce food loss and waste and to promote circular resource use increasingly intersect with food safety goals. Our research applies systems-based approaches to evaluate trade-offs and synergies among food safety, environmental sustainability, and circular food system objectives.

Anticipating & Managing Climate Change Impacts on Agri-Food Systems

Climate change is altering the environmental conditions that govern microbial behavior, exposure pathways, and human responses across agri-food systems. Our work focuses on understanding these dynamics and on developing forecasting and early-warning tools that link environmental change to food safety and public health outcomes.

Advancing Food System Equity

Foodborne illness and food system disruptions disproportionately affect under-resourced and marginalized communities. By integrating epidemiological, environmental, and food system data, we study how structural conditions shape exposure, vulnerability, and access to safer food systems.
