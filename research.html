Research

The MODES Lab examines food safety and public health through the lens of agri-food systems, linking environmental conditions, microbial ecology, epidemiology, and decision-making.

Ecology and Control of Pathogenic and Spoilage Organisms

Pathogenic and spoilage organisms move through agri-food systems via interconnected ecological and epidemiologic processes shaped by environmental conditions, agricultural practices, infrastructure, and human behavior.

Our research examines how these organisms persist, spread, and interact with production environments through processing and distribution, and ultimately how those dynamics shape food safety outcomes.

Foodborne Disease Epidemiology

A central focus of the lab is understanding how foodborne hazards translate into human illness and how epidemiologic evidence can guide prevention. We study the distribution and determinants of foodborne disease across populations, production systems, and environments.

By integrating epidemiologic methods with environmental, microbiological, and food systems data, we aim to generate evidence that supports surveillance, source attribution, intervention design, and policy.

Building Safe, Circular Food Systems

Food safety, circularity, and sustainability goals increasingly intersect. Interventions designed to improve one domain can create unintended consequences when considered in isolation.

Our work applies systems-based approaches to evaluate trade-offs and synergies among food safety, environmental sustainability, and circular food system objectives.

Climate Change and Food System Risk

Climate change is altering the environmental conditions that govern microbial behavior, exposure pathways, and infrastructure reliability across agri-food systems. Shifts in temperature, precipitation, and extreme weather can change contamination risk, spoilage, and resilience.

We are interested in understanding those dynamics and in developing forecasting and early-warning approaches that connect environmental change to food safety and public health outcomes.

Food System Equity

Foodborne illness and food system disruptions disproportionately affect under-resourced and marginalized communities. Our research integrates epidemiologic, environmental, and social information to understand disparities in exposure, vulnerability, and outcomes across agri-food systems.
