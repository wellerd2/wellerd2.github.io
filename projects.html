Current work

Projects

This page focuses on research directions that are active now or that define the lab’s near-term program. The wording below is ready to use as-is, but these blocks are also easy to revise as projects evolve.

Co-Managing Food Systems for Competing Aims

The lab develops tools and guidance that can help food system actors co-manage operations for food safety, sustainability, and economic and climate resilience. Current work is quantifying trade-offs between conservation practices and chemical and microbial hazard mitigation on urban farms and translating those findings into data-driven guidance for growers and extension professionals.

Disparities in Foodborne Disease Exposure and Burden

This work examines disparities in foodborne pathogen exposure, illness incidence, and outbreak risk across populations in the United States. Current analyses use novel quantitative approaches to identify structural, environmental, and geographic drivers of unequal disease burden and to better connect surveillance data with questions of equity and prevention.

Modeling Microbial Dynamics from Farm to Fork

The lab models how foodborne pathogens and spoilage organisms disperse, grow, and decline across the food system from pre-harvest through harvest and storage. Current work includes modeling pathogen dynamics on frozen produce during long-term storage and bacterial transfer from environmental reservoirs and equipment to produce during harvest.

Ecology of Foodborne and Antimicrobial-Resistant Pathogens

This project area investigates how foodborne pathogens and antimicrobial-resistant bacteria persist and move through freshwater, soil, and agricultural environments. Current work links land use, hydrology, and water management practices to pathogen occurrence in irrigation water and downstream contamination risk for produce.

User-Centered Decision Tools for On-Farm Food Safety

The lab develops practical, interpretable pipelines that use statistical and machine learning methods to identify farm- and water source–specific scenarios where contamination risk is most likely to occur. A major emphasis is making model outputs transparent, decision-relevant, and usable by growers, industry partners, and extension professionals.

Quantitative Risk Assessment and Attribution of Foodborne Illness

This work applies quantitative microbial risk assessment and source attribution models to identify likely sources of foodborne illness and evaluate control strategies. Current efforts focus on pathogens such as Campylobacter and Listeria monocytogenes and on using attribution results to inform more targeted prevention.
