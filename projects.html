Co-managing Urban Farms for Chemical and Microbial Food Safety, Sustainability, and Economic Resiliency

Urban farms often operate under resource constraints and on land with complex histories, including legacy contamination. At the same time, growers must manage microbial food safety risks. These risks are not evenly distributed and can vary substantially within a single farm.

This project examines how chemical and microbial hazards co-occur across urban farm environments and how they relate to land use history, environmental conditions, and management practices. A key focus is understanding how highly localized risk patterns, competing farm missions, and resource constraints complicate standard food safety approaches.

The goal is to identify approaches that reduce risk while still fitting the different missions, constraints, and day-to-day realities of urban farms.

Ongoing Projects

  • Copper fungicides and irrigation water quality. This project evaluates whether copper-based fungicides used in agricultural systems reduce microbial contamination in irrigation water, and how those effects vary across environmental conditions.
  • Pathogen transfer during harvest and postharvest handling. This work examines how pathogens move from environmental sources to produce and through postharvest environments, with a focus on identifying the conditions and pathways that drive contamination and strategies to reduce it.
  • Modeling pathogen survival on frozen produce. This work models how pathogens survive on frozen produce under different conditions, with the goal of understanding how storage and handling affect potential risk.
  • Changing diagnostic practices and disease epidemiology. This work examines how changes in diagnostic testing affect what can be concluded from surveillance data and how trends in disease are interpreted over time.
