Co-managing Chemical and Microbial Risks on Urban Farms

Urban farms often operate under resource constraints and on land with complex histories, including legacy contamination. At the same time, growers must manage microbial food safety risks. These risks are not evenly distributed and can vary substantially within a single farm.

This project examines how chemical and microbial hazards co-occur across urban farm environments and how they relate to land use history, environmental conditions, and management practices. A key focus is understanding how highly localized risk patterns, competing farm missions, and resource constraints complicate standard food safety approaches.

The goal is to identify management strategies that reduce risk while remaining feasible in systems where multiple objectives need to be balanced.

Interpreting Foodborne Disease Trends When Diagnostics Change

Reported trends in foodborne illness are often used to track progress and guide decisions. At the same time, diagnostic practices have changed, especially with increased use of culture-independent tests, altering how cases are detected and reported.

This project examines how these changes affect observed trends by analyzing surveillance data alongside information on diagnostic practices. We compare patterns across pathogens, regions, and time periods to identify where trends are comparable and where they are not.

The goal is to clarify when changes in reported incidence reflect changes in disease and when they reflect changes in detection.

Ongoing Projects

  • Copper fungicides and pathogen reduction in irrigation water. This work evaluates whether copper-based fungicides used in agricultural systems reduce microbial contamination in irrigation water, and how treatment effectiveness varies across conditions.
  • Modeling pathogen transfer in packing environments. This work models how pathogens move between produce, food contact surfaces, and wash water in packing environments to better understand which pathways drive contamination.
  • Pathogen transfer from environment to produce. This work examines how pathogens move from environmental sources to produce under field conditions, including the role of irrigation, wildlife, and surface contamination.
